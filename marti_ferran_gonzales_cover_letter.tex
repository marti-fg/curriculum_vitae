Dear \recipientname,\par

My experience in Formula Student and at CUPRA Racing has helped me identify the engineering work I enjoy most: translating physical behaviour into numerical models and understanding how well those models represent the real vehicle. I am applying to the \company\hspace{1mm}Graduate Programme with Simulator Development as my first preference and Off-Car Systems as my second, bringing experience in vehicle modelling, embedded control and track testing.

At CUPRA Racing, I developed a two-track vehicle model from scratch in MATLAB/Simulink as part of my bachelor’s thesis, using it to support yaw control algorithm development before vehicle testing. I compared its response with track data, achieving good agreement in steady state manoeuvres, although the model still needed work to reproduce transient behaviour accurately. Independently, I also developed a similar model in Python. In Formula Student, I worked with a point-mass lap-time simulator, using yaw moment diagram results to parametrise it and achieve a closer match to the real car’s performance. These projects strengthened my interest in vehicle modelling and taught me to assess the assumptions and conditions that determine how confidently simulation results can support engineering decisions.

I would like to apply my modelling experience to driver-in-the-loop simulation and learn how driver feedback is used in simulator development. My current MSc studies in Autonomous Vehicle Dynamics and Control at Cranfield and Aeronautical Engineering at UPC are extending my knowledge of dynamic systems and control.

After using MATLAB and Python for Formula Student data analysis, and WinTAX at CUPRA, I developed my own desktop data acquisition application with AI coding agents. I defined the requirements and interface, directed development and tested the software. It supports WinTAX data import, downloads from the team’s Vector logger and acquisition table configuration. Tested with recorded data and the physical logger, the first release is currently being evaluated by a Formula Student team member. Using the same approach, I also developed a double wishbone suspension kinematics tool with checks for component interference and joint travel limits.

At CUPRA Racing, I also modified Audi RS3 TCR gateway and dashboard software to support updated power steering maps. Contributing to Audi’s Formula 1 programme would let me apply that experience and learn more about the areas of engineering I enjoy most: simulation, vehicle dynamics and software development.

I would welcome the opportunity to discuss my projects and how I could contribute to the team.
