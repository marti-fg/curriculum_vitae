Dear \recipientname,\par
\setlength{\parskip}{8pt}

My experience in Formula Student and at CUPRA Racing has helped me identify the engineering work I enjoy most: translating physical behaviour into numerical models and understanding how well those models represent the real vehicle. I am applying to the \company\hspace{1mm}Graduate Programme with Simulator Development as my first preference and Off-Car Systems as my second, bringing experience in vehicle modelling, embedded control and track testing.

At CUPRA Racing, I developed a two-track vehicle dynamics model from scratch in MATLAB/Simulink as part of my bachelor’s thesis, using it to support yaw control development before vehicle testing. I compared its response with track data, achieving good agreement in steady-state manoeuvres while recognising the need for further validation under transient conditions. Independently, I also developed a similar model in Python. In Formula Student, I used point-mass lap-time simulation and improved the parametrisation linking yaw moment diagrams to the simulator’s vehicle acceleration limits. These projects strengthened my interest in vehicles modelling and taught me to asses the assumptions and conditions that determine how confidently simulation results can support engineering decisions.

I now want to build on that foundation in a driver-in-the-loop environment, learning how vehicle models, motion cueing and driver feedback are integrated into a useful engineering tool. My current MSc in Autonomous Vehicle Dynamics and Control at Cranfield, alongside my MSc in Aeronautical Engineering at UPC, is extending my knowledge of dynamic systems and control. I would welcome the opportunity to apply that knowledge to simulator development, testing and system integration.

My interest in Off-Car Systems comes from working with the data and tools that support vehicle development. After using MATLAB and Python for Formula Student data analysis, and WinTAX at CUPRA, I began developing my own desktop acquisition application with AI coding agents. I defined the product requirements and interface, directed development and tested the resulting workflows. The first release supports WinTAX data import, data download from the team’s Vector logger and acquisition-table configuration. I have tested it with recorded data and the physical logger; one Formula Student team member is currently evaluating it before a wider rollout. This has given me practical experience of turning an engineering need into a tool and improving it through user feedback.

Audi also has a direct connection to my previous work: at CUPRA Racing, I modified Audi TCR gateway and dashboard software to support updated power-steering maps. Contributing to Audi’s Formula 1 programme would allow me to build on that experience while developing in the areas that most interest me: simulation, vehicle dynamics and engineering software.

I would welcome the opportunity to discuss my projects and how I could contribute to the team.

Yours faithfully,\par
